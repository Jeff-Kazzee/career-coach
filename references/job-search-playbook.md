# Job Search Playbook

## What this reference is for

Design and troubleshoot a job search from target definition through applications, outreach, interviews, offers, and follow-up. It prevents high-volume activity from hiding a broken strategy.

## When the agent should read it

Read this for Job Search Mode, current-opening searches, application planning, outreach, tracker design, low response rates, offer evaluation, or negotiation preparation.

## The concrete process

### 1. Define a target-role hypothesis

Write one sentence:

> I am targeting [role family and level] in [industry or problem space] where I can use [credible strengths], under [location, schedule, access, and income constraints].

Include adjacent titles and exclusions. A search that targets “anything remote” is not yet a target.

### 2. Build a current requirement map

When live research is available:

1. Use BLS and O*NET for occupation-level tasks, work context, education, and skill vocabulary.
2. Collect a representative set of current postings for the target geography, level, and work mode.
3. Save the full posting text and date because listings change or disappear.
4. Count recurring requirements without pretending the sample represents the whole market.
5. Separate requirements into common, occasional, and employer-specific.
6. Mark genuine legal, licensing, clearance, physical, location, or work-authorization gates.

State the search date, geography, filters, and sample limitations.

### 3. Create a company and channel strategy

Use a mix appropriate to the field:

- Employer career sites
- General or specialist job boards
- Public-sector portals
- Staffing or recruiting firms
- Professional associations
- Alumni, community, or peer networks
- Apprenticeship or internship directories
- Open-source or portfolio communities
- Direct prospecting for small firms or freelance buyers

Do not claim one board is universally best. Track which channels produce qualified conversations.

Search-query patterns:

```text
("target title" OR "adjacent title") AND (skill OR domain) AND location
site:company-domain.example/careers "target title"
"target title" "remote" -senior -manager
"target title" apprenticeship OR trainee OR associate
```

Adapt syntax to the search tool. Do not bypass platform rules or scrape where prohibited.

### 4. Set a quality floor and capacity ceiling

The weekly plan needs both:

- **Quality floor:** minimum fit, truthful evidence, readable materials, complete application, saved posting.
- **Capacity ceiling:** maximum applications and tailoring load sustainable with the user's time and energy.

A smaller batch of comparable, well-targeted applications is more diagnostically useful than random volume.

### 5. Track every serious application

Use `assets/job-application-tracker-template.csv`. At minimum record:

- Company, role, source, URL, and posting date
- Date found and applied
- Saved job description
- Fit level and major gaps
- Resume and cover-letter versions
- Contact and follow-up
- Stage, dates, outcome, and red flags

Never put sensitive identifiers, passwords, medical details, or unnecessary benefits information in the tracker.

### 6. Tailor truthfully

For each role:

1. Identify the top three to five requirements.
2. Map each to existing evidence.
3. Reorder or rewrite relevant bullets using the employer's natural terminology only where accurate.
4. Add missing context or proof; do not add invented claims.
5. Follow requested file type and application instructions.
6. Preserve a master resume and version names.

A cover letter is most useful when required, when the role is high value, when motivation or a transition needs explanation, or when the user has a strong specific connection. It should add evidence and reasoning, not repeat the resume.

### 7. Make outreach concrete

Every outreach action needs:

- A specific person or role
- A real reason for contacting them
- One concise point of relevance
- A small ask
- A reasonable follow-up plan

Example informational message:

```text
Subject: Question about [team/role/domain]

Hi [Name]—I found your work on [specific item] while researching [target field]. I am building evidence in [relevant skill/project], and your transition from [A] to [B] stood out. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about what junior candidates commonly misunderstand about this work? No referral ask—I am trying to sharpen my target and proof.
```

Example post-application message:

```text
Hi [Name]—I applied for [Role] on [date]. The role's focus on [specific need] matches my work on [brief evidence]. I attached no extra files here; I only wanted to make the connection and point to [portfolio link] if useful. Thank you for considering my application.
```

Do not mass-send synthetic intimacy. Respect opt-outs and platform limits.

### 8. Diagnose the funnel

Use a batch of reasonably comparable applications before drawing conclusions. Review:

| Stage | Likely first checks |
|---|---|
| Few suitable roles | target, titles, geography, constraints, market demand |
| Found but not submitted | application burden, perfectionism, access, missing materials |
| Submitted but no screens | fit, resume clarity, proof, timing, keyword alignment, channel |
| Screens but no interviews | pitch, logistics, compensation, requirements, communication |
| Interviews but no offers | stories, technical readiness, role fit, portfolio, questions |
| Offers but no acceptable choice | pay, conditions, access, stability, negotiation, risk |

Do not promise that changing one variable will produce a job. Run a small test and review results.

### 9. Handle current openings correctly

When the user asks for current jobs:

- Use permitted live-search or job-search tools.
- Open the employer's official posting when possible.
- Verify that the role still appears active.
- Report the access date, location, work mode, stated pay, and source.
- Quote requirements sparingly and accurately.
- Never invent an opening or infer that an aggregator listing is still live.
- Warn that posting status and terms can change after research.

### 10. Screen for scams

Treat these as serious red flags:

- Payment to obtain a job, training, leads, or equipment
- Gift cards, crypto, money transfer, or reshipping tasks
- A check followed by instructions to buy equipment or return funds
- Recruiters using lookalike domains or only text/chat without verifiable identity
- Requests for sensitive identity or bank data before a legitimate hiring stage
- Pay dramatically disconnected from duties with no credible explanation
- A role absent from the employer's official site when it should be present

Verify independently through the employer's official domain and known contact channels. Do not use contact details supplied only by the suspicious sender.

### 11. Compare offers as complete systems

Use a weighted matrix with user-chosen priorities:

- Guaranteed compensation and realistic variable pay
- Hours, schedule control, commute, remote terms, and accessibility
- Benefits and out-of-pocket costs
- Employment classification and stability
- Manager, team, role clarity, and workload
- Learning, portfolio value, advancement, and exit options
- Contract restrictions, intellectual-property terms, and risk
- Culture evidence, not slogans

Salary and market comparisons must use current, geographically relevant sources. For contracts, taxes, benefits, immigration, or legal terms, help prepare questions and recommend a qualified professional.
