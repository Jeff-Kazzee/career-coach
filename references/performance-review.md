# Work Performance, Visibility, Promotion, and Raise Preparation

## What this reference is for

Help a user perform more effectively in an existing job, contract, internship, apprenticeship, volunteer role, or recurring client relationship while creating evidence for reviews, expanded scope, promotion, or compensation discussions.

## When the agent should read it

Read this in Performance Mode, for manager communication, role ambiguity, feedback, prioritization, accomplishment tracking, promotion planning, raise preparation, meetings, or workplace conflict.

## The concrete process

### 1. Map expectations before optimizing performance

Collect:

- Role description, contract, goals, review criteria, and current priorities
- Manager's stated expectations and examples of strong work
- Actual work assigned versus formal role
- Stakeholders, decision rights, dependencies, and recurring deadlines
- Recent feedback and consequences
- User's capacity, accommodations, schedule, and communication constraints

Create an expectations map:

| Outcome | Quality bar | Deadline/cadence | Stakeholder | Evidence | Current risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

When expectations conflict, prepare a prioritization question instead of advising the user to silently absorb all work.

### 2. Establish a weekly accomplishment log

Use `assets/promotion-evidence-log-template.md`, even when promotion is not the immediate goal. Record:

- Work shipped
- Problem or need
- User's contribution
- Scope and collaborators
- Result or current status
- Quality, speed, cost, risk, access, or customer effect
- Positive feedback
- Links or artifacts permitted by confidentiality rules
- Competencies demonstrated

Do not copy confidential customer data, source code, internal metrics, or sensitive messages into an external log.

### 3. Improve role clarity with a manager

Prepare a concise alignment message:

```text
I want to confirm priorities for [period]. I currently understand the top outcomes as:
1. [Outcome]
2. [Outcome]
3. [Outcome]

Given [new request or constraint], I can complete [A] and [B] by [date], or move [C]. Which tradeoff best matches your priority?
```

Capture decisions in an appropriate follow-up note. Do not create a covert evidence dossier; keep ordinary work records professional and proportionate.

### 4. Build a visibility system

Visibility means decision-makers can understand useful work—not self-promotion without substance. Use:

- Brief weekly updates: done, impact, next, risk, decision needed
- Demos or walkthroughs
- Clear project documentation
- Credit that accurately names collaborators
- Early escalation of risks
- Stakeholder feedback

Example update:

```markdown
Done: [shipped output]
Effect: [verified result or risk reduced]
Next: [next milestone]
Risk: [specific block]
Decision needed: [owner and date]
```

### 5. Ask for actionable feedback

Avoid “Any feedback?” Prefer:

- What would move this work from acceptable to strong?
- Which part of my communication creates the most rework?
- What responsibility would you need to see me handle consistently before considering the next level?
- Can you point to one recent example where I should have made a different tradeoff?

Record the request, response, action taken, and later evidence.

### 6. Diagnose performance problems

Distinguish:

- Skill or knowledge gap
- Unclear expectations
- Competing priorities or overload
- Missing tools, access, or authority
- Communication or visibility gap
- Quality-control failure
- Relationship or conflict problem
- Accessibility mismatch
- Organizational dysfunction or bad-faith management

Do not assume every performance issue is fixable by working harder. When the environment is unsafe, discriminatory, retaliatory, or legally questionable, help organize facts and questions and recommend qualified support.

### 7. Prepare important meetings

Before a meeting, define:

- Desired decision or outcome
- Facts and evidence
- Stakeholder concerns
- Tradeoffs and recommendation
- Questions
- Boundaries and follow-up owner

For conflict, use observable behavior and effect:

> When [specific event] happened, [work effect] followed. I need [clear request] so we can meet [shared outcome].

Avoid diagnosing another person's motives. Preserve records lawfully and appropriately.

### 8. Assess promotion readiness

Build a side-by-side case:

| Next-level expectation | Current evidence | Repeated or one-off? | Missing proof | Next assignment |
|---|---|---|---|---|

A promotion case is stronger when it shows sustained next-level scope and organizational need, not only tenure or effort. Also assess whether the organization has budget, headcount, level definitions, and a decision process.

Ask the manager early:

> What specific outcomes and scope would demonstrate readiness for [level], who participates in that decision, and when are decisions made?

### 9. Prepare a raise or scope discussion

Use:

- Current responsibilities and expanded scope
- Results and repeated evidence
- Current role/level expectations
- Current, geographically relevant compensation research
- A clear request
- Alternatives if base pay is constrained: title, scope, schedule, professional development, bonus structure, review date, equipment, or other valued terms

Do not treat crowd-sourced compensation as definitive. State source dates and limitations.

Example structure:

```text
Over [period], my scope expanded from [A] to [B]. The strongest results are [evidence]. I would like to discuss adjusting [compensation/title/scope] to [specific request] based on the role I am performing and current market evidence. What is the decision process and timeline?
```

### 10. Protect sustainability

Look for:

- Chronic unpaid expansion of scope
- Repeated urgency without prioritization
- Workload beyond agreed hours
- Accommodations not functioning in practice
- Deteriorating health or severe burnout
- Client or employer dependence that creates unsafe financial risk

Offer minimum, standard, and stretch performance plans. Severe burnout, crisis, legal disputes, discrimination, or accommodation enforcement require appropriate professionals; the coach can help prepare a timeline, questions, and communication.
